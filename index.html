The Problem

The risk you can feel but can't quite prove

No single line item is wrong. But the timeline keeps slipping, the numbers don't quite reconcile, and every status update sounds more confident than the program actually feels. Four patterns explain most of that gap.

01

Selection made on features, not fit

Systems get chosen off a demo, not a rigorous fit against real business process and future operating model.

02

No independent executive governance

Steering committees rubber-stamp vendor and SI recommendations without an advisor solely representing the client's interest.

03

Process mapping skipped or rushed

Teams configure around legacy habits instead of redesigning process — locking in inefficiency for another decade.

04

Change management as an afterthought

Adoption, training, and communication get compressed at the end of the project, right when it matters most.
